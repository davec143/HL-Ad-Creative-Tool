How to use this

  1. Pick the product. Search the catalogue by name or SKU and click the result you want. The tool pulls that product's own photograph from Shopify, so the hardware in the finished ad is the real thing rather than an approximation. If the product has no image on file, or it isn't in Shopify at all, switch to the URL tab and paste a direct link to a hosted image instead.

  2. Check the stock chip before you go further. Every search result shows its current inventory. A red chip means zero units on hand — building the creative is still fine, but don't put budget behind it until it restocks, because an ad pointing at an unbuyable product spends its whole budget for nothing.

  3. Write the message, or have it written for you. You can fill in the two headline lines, the proof points (or, on Spotlight, the one supporting sentence), the CTA wording and the support phone and email yourself. Or type the angle you're after — who it's for, what it should emphasise — and press Write it for me; Claude reads the product's own listing and drafts all of it, which you're then free to edit. Either way, proof points must carry real figures from the product sheet. If a number isn't documented, leave it out rather than guessing at it.

  4. Describe the shot. Say plainly what is physically happening in the photograph — who is in frame, what they're doing, where it takes place. One concrete sentence gets you further than three vague ones, and the product itself carries over from the image you picked, so there's no need to describe it.

  5. Choose the template. The five here are the five in the brand kit, grouped by the pillar each one serves. Pillar A, Pro-Trust, holds three: Template 1, Collage Hero is the violet-panel workhorse for installers and specifiers; Template 2, Speech-Bubble Hero drops the panel for a full-bleed photo with a white card over the top third, for service and reassurance angles; Template 5, Glow Room Hero is the pro brand look, where the LED-lit room fills the frame, the product sits in front of it, every word is white, and one supporting sentence replaces the three proof points. Pillar B, Promo Urgency, holds Template 3, Discount Deadline — the offer figure in gold as the biggest thing on the frame, plus a deadline line, the only template with that field. Pillar C, Aesthetic Lifestyle, holds Template 4, Styled Room Hero, the cream look for homeowners, which deliberately rules out job sites, tool belts and work gloves — avoiding that imagery is the entire reason it exists. Picking a template changes which fields appear, so choose it first.

  6. Generate, and give it a few minutes. The square renders first, then the portrait and landscape versions are built from that square rather than from scratch. That's what keeps all three genuinely the same image instead of three separate designs. Leave the tab open while it works.

  7. Collect the results. If the Drive box was ticked, the three files are already sitting in a new dated folder inside Claude + Higgsfield Ad Creatives, cropped to exactly 1080×1080, 1080×1920 and 1200×628 and named after their dimensions — use those, they are Ads Manager ready. The Higgsfield links in the Output panel are the uncropped originals, kept in case you want to re-crop differently.

  8. If something's off, change one thing and run it again. Adjust the scene, or a single headline line, rather than rewriting everything at once — it's far easier to tell what actually moved the result.

Before your first run: generating happens on your own Claude account, so you need the Higgsfield connector added under Settings → Connectors, with credit on that Higgsfield account — a Claude subscription alone does not cover the rendering. Shopify is optional and only powers the catalog search. If either is missing, a banner at the top of this page says which one and how to add it.

No Higgsfield? Use the prompt pack. Build the prompt pack instead under step 03 turns whatever you have written into the three exact, brand-locked prompts, in the right order, with the settings each one needs. Paste that into a Claude chat that does have Higgsfield, or send it to someone who does, and the result is identical to pressing Generate here.

The brand system is already applied. Colours, Montserrat, the gold pill, the layout order and the photography rules are built into what this tool sends — you don't need to describe any of it. The fields above are only the parts that change per ad.

The generator never draws the logo. Every prompt reserves a corner and orders it left completely empty, with no wordmark or brand lettering anywhere on the frame. Your real logo file is then laid into that reserved space while each image is cropped to size — the white lockup on Templates 1, 3, 4 and 5, the black one inside Template 2’s white card. That means the Higgsfield links in the Output panel show the raw render with an empty corner: the finished files are the ones in Drive or behind the save buttons, and they are the only ones fit to publish.

Where the files land. Each run makes its own folder named date, then product — for example 2026-09-16 EZDim Pro — inside the shared Claude + Higgsfield Ad Creatives folder. Running the same product twice makes two folders rather than overwriting the first, so nothing is ever lost; tidy up the ones you don’t keep.

Reels aren't built here. The video ad is a separate deliverable — 1080×1920, 5–10 seconds, CTA or contact at the end. Ask Claude in chat for that one.

Before you hand it over

  • Three files, one image. Open all three side by side — the same photograph, the same wording and the same colours should appear in each.

  • Logo placed cleanly. The logo itself is the real file, so its letterforms are correct by construction. What to check is the space around it: that the generator actually left that corner empty, that nothing is peeking out from behind the lockup, and that it has not drawn a second wordmark somewhere else on the frame.

  • Publishing the finished files. The Drive copies and the save buttons give you 1080×1080, 1080×1920 and 1200×628 with the logo applied. The Higgsfield links are raw renders with an empty logo corner — never send those to Ads Manager.

  • Proof points specific. Every figure traced to the product sheet, never estimated to fill a line.

  • Landing page in stock. Check the SKU in Shopify first; an ad pointing at zero inventory spends its whole budget for nothing.

  • Reel handled separately. 1080×1920, 5–10 seconds, CTA or contact at the end, and nothing burned into the top or bottom 250 pixels.

Uploading a file from your computer: a published page can't pass local files to the image generator — the browser sandbox blocks it. Put the image somewhere with a public link first (a Shopify product image is easiest), or ask Claude in chat and attach it there.
