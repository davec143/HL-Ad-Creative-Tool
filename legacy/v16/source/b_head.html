How to use this

  1. Pick the product. Search the catalogue by name or SKU and click the result you want. The tool pulls that product's own photograph from Shopify, so the hardware in the finished ad is the real thing rather than an approximation. If the product has no image on file, or it isn't in Shopify at all, switch to the URL tab and paste a direct link to a hosted image instead.

  2. Check the stock chip before you go further. Every search result shows its current inventory. A red chip means zero units on hand — building the creative is still fine, but don't put budget behind it until it restocks, because an ad pointing at an unbuyable product spends its whole budget for nothing.

  3. Write the message, or have it written for you. You can fill in the two headline lines, the proof points, the CTA wording, and the support phone and email yourself. Or type the angle you're after — who it's for, what it should emphasise — and press Write it for me; Claude reads the product's own listing and drafts all of it, which you're then free to edit. Either way, proof points must carry real figures from the product sheet. If a number isn't documented, leave it out rather than guessing at it.

  4. Describe the shot. Say plainly what is physically happening in the photograph — who is in frame, what they're doing, where it takes place. One concrete sentence gets you further than three vague ones, and the product itself carries over from the image you picked, so there's no need to describe it.

  5. Choose the template first. It sets which fields apply. Template 1, Collage Hero is always-on brand for pros and installers. Template 2, Speech-Bubble Hero is for B2B and contractor prospecting, with the phone and email footer. Template 3, Discount Deadline is for sales, retargeting and seasonal deadlines; it swaps proof points for an offer and a deadline line, which you write yourself. Template 4, Styled Room Hero is the lifestyle look for homeowners, and it deliberately rules out job sites, tool belts and work gloves. Template 5, Glow Room Hero is the “Built For Professionals” look: a dark interior lit by LED strips, big white headline and subheadline, and the glowing product in the foreground.

  6. Generate, and give it a few minutes. The square renders first, then the portrait and landscape versions are built from that square rather than from scratch. That's what keeps all three genuinely the same image instead of three separate designs. Leave the tab open while it works.

  7. Read the checks on each file. Every finished file shows where the logo landed and a Text & logo check: Claude reads the image and compares every line against what you typed, looks for stray words or a second logo, and checks nothing touches the real logo. A red note means don't use that file yet — press Regenerate this size on it (2 credits, rebuilt from the same square) or Regenerate the whole set on the square.

  8. Take the finished files. After the renders come back, the tool crops each one to exactly 1080×1080, 1080×1920 and 1200×628 and places the real HitLights logo on it at the fixed Logo Grid position for that template and size \u2014 the same spot every time, never moved to find space. If something is wrong under the logo, that file gets a red note instead. The three finished images appear under Output with a download button each, and — if the box in step 03 is ticked — in a new Drive folder named date + product name inside Claude + Higgsfield Ad Creatives. The raw render links are the generator's own output, before cropping and the logo.

  9. If something's off, change one thing and run it again. Adjust the scene, or a single headline line, rather than rewriting everything at once — it's far easier to tell what actually moved the result.

Before your first run: generating happens on your own Claude account, so you need the Higgsfield connector added under Settings → Connectors, with credit on that Higgsfield account — a Claude subscription alone does not cover the rendering. A set costs 6 credits; the balance shows under Generate. The text & logo check uses your Claude usage and asks permission the first time. Shopify is optional and only powers the catalog search. Google Drive is what saves the finished sizes into the shared Claude + Higgsfield Ad Creatives folder; without it the renders still appear here. If Higgsfield or Shopify is missing, a banner at the top of this page says which one and how to add it.

No Higgsfield? Use the prompt pack. Build the prompt pack instead under step 03 turns whatever you have written into the three exact, brand-locked prompts, in the right order, with the settings each one needs. Paste that into a Claude chat that does have Higgsfield, or send it to someone who does, and the result is identical to pressing Generate here.

The brand system is already applied. Colours, Montserrat, the gold pill, the layout order and the photography rules are built into what this tool sends — you don't need to describe any of it. The fields above are only the parts that change per ad. All five HitLights Ad Templates are built in, each with its own palette, layout and button style.

Reels aren't built here. The video ad is a separate deliverable — 1080×1920, 5–10 seconds, CTA or contact at the end. Ask Claude in chat for that one.

Before you hand it over

  • Every check green. Text & logo check passed on all three, no red notes. Recent sets keeps the record.

  • Three files, one image. Open all three side by side — the same photograph, the same wording and the same colours should appear in each.

  • Proof points specific. Every figure traced to the product sheet, never estimated to fill a line.

  • Landing page in stock. Check the SKU in Shopify first; an ad pointing at zero inventory spends its whole budget for nothing.

  • Reel handled separately. 1080×1920, 5–10 seconds, CTA or contact at the end, and nothing burned into the top or bottom 250 pixels.
